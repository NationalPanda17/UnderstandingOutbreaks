# Testing the site with three readers

Nobody outside the project has used the site yet. This is a script for a first test: three people, about 25 minutes
each, one at a time. It needs no software and collects nothing automatically. You watch, they talk.

## Who to ask

1. **A journalist** who has written about health or data at least once.
2. **Someone who works in public health or epidemiology.**
3. **A general reader** with no background in either.

Do not test with people who helped build the site.

## Before you start

- Use the live site on the reader's own device (one of the three on a phone).
- Say: "I am testing the site, not you. Please say what you are thinking as you go. I will not help unless you are
  stuck for more than two minutes."
- Do not explain the site first. Start on the home page.
- Ask permission before recording. Notes are enough.

## Tasks

Read each task aloud, exactly as written. Stop a task after four minutes.

| # | Task | What it tests | Done when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "In your own words, what is this site and who is behind it?" (30 seconds on the home page, no clicking) | First impression, trust | They describe official reports gathered in one place; note what they say about who runs it |
| 2 | "Find how many measles cases were most recently reported for a country you choose, and tell me how recent that number is." | Search, country page, the date of a figure | They state a number **and** its period |
| 3 | "Find an outbreak that is being reported on right now. What do official sources say about it, and where does that come from?" | Outbreak list and page, source links | They open an outbreak page and reach an official notice |
| 4 | "You want to quote one of these numbers in an article or a report. Show me what you would copy." | Cite panel, story kit | They find the citation or the story kit |
| 5 | "Is this number likely to change later? How can you tell?" (on any chart they have open) | Incomplete-period marking, revisions | They mention that recent periods are incomplete or revised |
| 6 | "Can this site tell you whether an outbreak will get worse?" | Whether the limits of the site are understood | They say no, or find the statement that it does not forecast |
| 7 | (Public health reader only) "Download the data behind this chart and tell me which source and case definition it uses." | Downloads, trace panel | They get a file and name the source and definition |

## What to write down

For each task: finished without help / finished with help / gave up; time taken; the first place they clicked; any
word they did not understand; anything they said they did not trust.

## Three closing questions

1. "What was the most confusing moment?"
2. "Would you rely on a number from this site? What would make you more sure?"
3. "What did you expect to find that was not there?"

## Reading the results

A problem two of the three readers hit is worth fixing before anything else. A problem one reader hit is worth a
second look. Task 6 matters most: if a reader comes away thinking the site predicts or warns, the wording on the
page they were on needs to change. File each finding as a GitHub issue with the task number and the reader's own
words. Do not record names.
